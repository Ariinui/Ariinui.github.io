The Zoramite Prayer

💡 Did you know that the Zoramite characterization of the teachings of Christ as "childishness" is likely a negative characterization of King Benjamin's teaching that disciples of Christ must become as "little children"?

The prophet Alma led a mission to the Zoramites in the land of Antionum around 74 B.C. Alma described a distinctive prayer offered by these dissident Zoramites. Once every week, one at a time, each worshipper would ascend a stand in the center of their place of worship, raise their hands toward heaven, and loudly repeat the same prayer (Alma 31:15-18). The Zoramite prayer is 222 words long and has 12 references to Deity. It mentions "God" five times, "Spirit" three times, "Christ" two times, and "Holy God" two times. The title "Holy God" is used only six times in the entire Book of Mormon, so one-third of those references are from this prayer. The word "elected" is used four times in this prayer and nowhere else in the Book of Mormon text. The word "childishness," used once, is also only found here. Interestingly, the words "tradition," "foolish traditions," and "handed down" are each used once, indicating that the concept of tradition is a message marker. The Zoramites' set prayer, which was given each time without variation, publicly proclaimed their sense of superiority and distinction from Nephite beliefs in Christ and His gospel. "Christ" is only mentioned to dismiss the Nephite belief in the Messiah and practices associated with Him. Key Insights The Zoramites attributed Nephite teachings about Christ to "the childishness of our brethren" (Alma 31:17). This reminds us of King Benjamin's teachings of the need for all to "become as little children " and believe in Christ (Mosiah 3:18). To be saved, each must come unto Christ and become " as a child, submissive, meek, humble, patient, full of love, willing to submit to all things which the Lord seeth fit to inflict upon him, even as a child doth submit to his father" (Mosiah 3:19). By characterizing Nephite teachings about Christ as "childishness," the Zoramites disparaged King Benjamin's teachings as foolishness, which they contrasted with their self-appointed status as "holy children" of God who were better than the Nephites. When we read the words of the Zoramites, we should contrast these words with other Book of Mormon passages that describe what it truly means to be children of God. This will help us to more fully appreciate how God loves His children, and how that love should be manifested in our own concern and service toward others (Alma 34:28-29).
